3 | 	Time flew, and the distance grew. About half a week was spent traveling on foot, covering perhaps a hundred miles or less. It wasn’t a race, but more or less an effort to keep Anastasia moving, whatever I meant by that. Though, for Wilmert, traveling was second nature. He liked walking, after all; it was kind of refreshing to walk in pitch-black smoke in the north, isn’t it? Though the area through which they traveled remained the same—forest, forest, and, you guessed it, more forest! A pity, if you ask me, though I only knew about cities of modern times, so I guess I’m not suitable to judge. It doesn’t matter anyway, dear reader, since the point was that not much had happened since I last spoke…until now.

4 | 	If anything, it was a cool night, almost lukewarm. Here, a campfire was built to cook something for the night, mostly ordinary rabbits that could be found anywhere. Besides, that’s really all they could find, oddly enough.

5 | 	““You don’t find it strange?” said Wilmert, in the midst of his thoughts as Anastasia roasts a rabbit, “You would think that you would see many more animals than…hares.”

6 | 	“They’re rabbits, not hares,” said Anastasia with slight annoyance at hearing this. Wilmert couldn’t care less, if anything.

7 | 	“But one is wilder than the other, no?””

8 | 	“That’s the thing,” she looks at the meat by rotating it, which still needed to be cooked, “we have animals and creatures. You probably have seen a lot of animals in the north of Yeropa because, unlike animals, creatures are people who resonate more with nature while trying to avoid anything urban.”

9 | 	“Interesting.” Wilmert took a sip of tea that was also brewed by her, “do they possess human intellect? Are there more than just these rabbit creatures?” He then realized that this would have been a good moment to write his thesis.

10 | 	“Mostly rabbits and crows are the ones I’ve dealt with—here you go, gotta eat—but I wouldn’t be surprised to see others, like fish people or fox people from down south. But, to answer your question, creatures are like humans with animalistic traits. Basically, they’re like us, but with additional characteristics that we don’t have—tails for example. In theory, they should be far superior to us since they’re ‘extraordinary,’ yet they aren’t. If anything, they would rather resort to their animalistic behaviors while mimicking humans. And, hey, if they’re not aggressive, then they coexist.”

11 | 	“I see, it’s rather unfortunate that we get told that these creatures are ought to eliminate us all.”

12 | 	Anastasia almost choked on her saliva, “What?! That’s utter nonsense!”

13 | 	““Well…” He took a moment to think. “Again, remember the group we are chasing after?”

14 | 	“That’s just straight up lying! Of all the creatures I dealt with back in Transcarpathia, not a single one was that utterly violent! If anything, we’re more aggressive than them, you know?”

15 | 	Wilmert sighed before taking another sip, “These days, no one really bothers to question anything—If it’s loud and logical, then maybe it’s true.”

16 | 	Anastasia takes a moment to think. Most of it was what she reiterated before—Creatures friendly, humans unlikely. But, maybe over there is different from back home. Something doesn’t add up, it’s got to be a misunderstanding that the Northerners cooked. But then again, if anything, maybe she was living under a rock.

17 | 	As the two took a moment to reflect, a loud bang could be heard from afar—about half a mile from their camp. Then a second, a third, followed by a spectacle of fireworks in various colors illuminating the crystal, sparkling night. Clearly a fight was happening, so, without a doubt, they ran to investigate. Loudness ensues, burn marks engraves. Two or more trees were affected. Sparks and spectacles fly with full colors; one nearly shot past Anastasia before Wilmert rescued her. In other words, it’s a whole festival of lights that replaced the audience’s joyful laughter with shouts of anger and fear.

18 | 	“Come out,” said a feminine voice that was oddly cheerful, “I can take a hundred more of you freaks with these shrooms, YEE-HAW!” accompanied by another blast from what seems to be her staff.

19 | 	“Is she so… high?” said Wilmert as both he and Anastasia were dumbfounded, “no sane individual would just kill for fun, unless that’s twisted coping?”

20 | 	“Whatever is happening,” said Anastasia, “she’s definitely out of her mind. Do something, Wilmert!”

21 | 	But before Wilmert could do something, another blast ensues, this time thrusting through their hiding spot, forcing them to dodge—they’re spotted.

22 | 	“So, these lads send out the experts, huh? I guess the northerners didn’t learn their lesson hard enough, alright~!” said the feminine voice, eager to continue fighting.

23 | This does not resonate with Anastasia, “What’s with you? Killing without purpose is pure insanity. FREGHSTICKHT! You have no business being alive!”

24 | 	And for the first time ever, her years of trying to understand magic were put to use. Anastasia levitated, stretching her arms as she closed her eyes. She looks up, speaks of an ancient language, akin to Latin if it were rural-centric, dear reader.

25 | 	“Saliva, sein…”

26 | As she slowly speaks them calmly, a hex diagram appears, followed by rotary blades spun rapidly as a charge beam enlarged. Eventually, everything seemed pitch-black as she says her final word before releasing the beam.

27 | 	“…Excelsior.”

28 | And to that, the feminine voice calmly states, “Oh shoot.”

29 | 	BOOM!

30 | The beam seemed to blast out in a radius of about a mile. And just like that, only nothingness remained afterward. Dear reader, Anastasia was fully unconscious. It was a spell on a grand scale she casted that resonated more on her rage from that supposed violent mania. Except, what caused this were creatures who annoyed that young woman’s way of life, so much so that annoying them felt like the best course of action. This would have been fine…have it not be that she smoked dried mushrooms, taking her behavior to the next level. Unfortunately, this is her old habit—an addict.

31 | Anyway, according to the next event from our guidebook, they are then taken as prisoners—Three miles away from the blast towards southwest. Hey, at least they get a short nap. Which, fun fact—Yeropa is still largely a forest place up north, except that one island mentioned earlier. Why I’m mentioning this? Because the place where they were held captive was a settlement of rabbit creatures.

32 | About two hundred occupants live here, ruled by a scholar of the north, akin to Wilmert’s study. It has a stone fortress in an otherwise wooden huts of living. This doesn’t matter to the rabbits as they only need food, of which there was plenty—grass. And since the forest is their home, they could decide to sleep anywhere if they want. As for the purpose of the huts? That’s rather the human part of them you should be asking them. Nonetheless, time passed for who knows how long before being taken to the scholar, “Who are you all?!” It was a woman, by the way, and each was given a moment to shine, with Wilmert and Anastasia calmly stating their backgrounds. And then there’s the shroom-addict girl.

33 | “Damn it, it’s you again—Melinda Meldilson, a witch who practiced black magic from the outskirts of northern Yeropa. I heard you abandoned your duty of segregating the cultures of the two down central, so why are you here? Have you come to accompany them to destroy Yeropa?” said the scholar, acting as rather annoyed by Melinda’s presence.

34 | “To hell with you, as if you give a damn,” said Melinda while not wanting to look at her, “I told you people many times that I’m not a witch! Witches are those of malice.”

35 | “And what differs from what you did by performing unsanitary, unholy rituals upon yourself and others?”

36 | “My goodness! You northerners speak like damn hypocrites, thinking with superiority complexes because of this whole culture of show and tell. I’m a priestess of the central Yeropa peninsula, formally seeing the sides of both north and south till I had enough, to which y’all send a messenger to excommunicate me because me sympathizing the ways of the south is considered ‘black magic.’”

37 | “Those who aren’t us are against us.”

38 | “Okay, and care to explain why these rabbit folk have no free will?”

39 | “SILENCE!” the scholar shouted, “My reasons for dealing with them are none of your concern.”

40 | “Yet again, the double meaning behind the northerner’s ideology of purity.”

41 | This resulted in a mild beating from some of the rabbit lads that saw the scholar as a divine entity. Shouts, tears, and anything in association. For Anastasia, this was a shock—Creatures obeying humans? Committing violence without resistance? This was never like that back home! Creatures didn’t hold morality standpoints, at least not to her understanding, so what’s happening? An evolution? Whoever this scholar is, she needs to be taught a lesson that violence is never the answer!

42 | “Don’t sweat it, Anastasia,” Melinda still foolishly smirks menacingly after being beaten, “she’s too powerful with their ideologies to not think twice. Mothertruckers should’ve erected her a pillar for being the most egotistical so far.” Another beating ensues.

43 | “Wait…” Anastasia wondered, “isn’t Wilmert part of them? Can’t you do something about it?”

44 | “Well,” Wilmert replied, “let’s just say that I, too, am rather shocked by this.”

45 | “And you don’t get a scratch for that?” says Melinda, looking disgruntled.

46 | Soon they were placed back in their stone jail cells. Melinda relaxed, Anastasia ponders, and Wilmert? He did not fret, for he took his journal to write out. Except, some sparks seemed to hover in and out.

47 | “Huh,” Melinda noticed, “a pseudo-telepathic writing? Who are you trying to cryptically communicate?”

48 | “I’m not much of a social person, Melinda, I’m simply writing my thesis.”

49 | “Then what is it about? My worst days being fresh out of college?” They chuckled.

50 | “Do all scholars have to do it, Melinda?” curiously asked Anastasia.

51 | “Heard about it, but I guess I was too cool to finish it.”

52 | “Ouch, talk about it…”

53 | “Never mind that, I’m bored.” Melinda got up to casually blast the cell’s wall, as if it were an ordinary Tuesday, opening a way out for them, “Just because she is one of the powerful folks doesn’t mean I have to sit here and do nothing. Come on, we gotta blast off!”

54 | Both Wilmert and Anastasia were dumbfounded, for a second time. Nevertheless, she quickly shook her head, “Actually, I can help us go quicker. Let’s see…what’s the other spell I remember…Saliva, Sar… Specscicus!” Three phantom horses appeared, almost like clouds that illuminated in rim-light. Melinda seems rather unamused, “I’d rather run, but okay.”

55 | And the race is on! Nothing was more important than getting out as it didn’t matter where, so long as long as they escaped the danger, and twenty miles per hour was the goal.

56 | “That’s the thing!” Anastasia yelled, “If the wind were on our side, we’d be flying in no time! You know any open valleys nearby? We can use them before our horses slowly decayed.”

57 | “They’re not for everyday use?” Melinda yells back.

58 | “No! I’d need to recast it on foot with my remaining energy! I have to concentrate after-“

59 | A light arrow flew near Anastasia’s forehead; those rabbits had joined the fight.

60 | “Rabbit archers—HOW?!” shouted Anastasia.

61 | “Surprised, lad? More fun for me!” Melinda cast a few ballistic-like rain bullets. There was a sense of exchanged fire between the two, “I’ve got your back, lad,” Wilmert said in a rush, casting a shield to defend the three—finally—willing to take action after writing. Anastasia, however, didn’t know what to do. For that, she only knew magics of support and grace, nothing suited for offense. Melinda was the opposite. But, soon after, their horses started to decay, so was the forest began to thin—an open valley for once! But far too late, unfortunately, as our heroes are surrounded in an arc-like formation, almost like a circle. As they take the high ground, all hope seems lost to them.

62 | Horns blew minutes after, the ground shook of a hundred feet, if not thousands—The army of rabbits emerges. Well, they’re rabbits, after all, so they reproduce really quick, dear reader. Was this really the end? Everyone stood still, arrows being readied. Say the word, and so shall it be. Then the scholar emerged from the crowd, having a rather concerned looked. Between her and Wilmert, there felt a connection of northerners’ pride before she vanished. It wasn’t clear what was said between them when combining the crowds’ grunt. Nevertheless, Wilmert told our heroes to ready themselves. Melinda was to take the offense, suggesting a deadlier rain of bullets. For him, he was to then serve as an an energy bank for an endless supply of hell to be let loose. The air became thick with uncertainty before the battle commenced—A spectacle of fireworks that could be seen from miles on end.

63 | Like raindrops from both directions, the attacks came from both sides. Anyone who attempted melee combat would be met by blockage from Wilmert’s other spell—A shield—that covered just enough for the three. But because they were only mortals, and their energy was finite. For Anastasia, she could do nothing but stare at the chaos that was soon to become a dire end. Seeing how slowly it unravels, it fueled her energy that was similar to first encountering Melinda. This was the grace skill to be used, “Saliva, Sein, Excelsior,” just as Wilmert’s energy was depleted.

64 | BOOM!

65 | All that could be seen was a bright light that would’ve blinded you if you looked at it directly. As before, the radius was about a mile, perhaps even half again as much since her energy was concentrated from her act of desperation, which felt like a matter of life or death. Dear reader, it would later become known in history in Yeropa as the rabbit massacre of 1404 as there was a significant reduction in the number of such creatures afterward.

66 | If anything, this marked possibly Anastasia’s way of reclaiming Yeropa’s harmony on a difficult path. As for the scholar? She has yet to be seen again.
