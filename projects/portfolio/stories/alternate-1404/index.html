Preface

PREFACE

Dear reader,

What you are about to read is a conflict that will echo for others to deal with as the centuries unfold. It is a tale that parallels your world from a different spelling. For instance, while you may call it ‘Europe,’ we call it ‘Yeropa.’ Yes, in it, we use a more phonetic pronunciation of our tongues, as we logically should. We curse in German, resonate like the Italians, and express our emotions similarly to the Spanish. In other words, in the world of Yeropa, there are no countries, but rather a large group of ethnicities united by their ideologies. Not only that, technology and mysticism, swords and magic, humans and creatures coexist. Here, we start our story in rural areas and slowly progress to a more urban city—Malta.

Why am I telling you this? Dear reader, I myself, Maria Shalashuska, the ambassador of the foreign entity who rules this world, am tasked with guiding you through our heroine’s journey from the Transcarpathian Mountains to Malta to eradicate the emerging force that will forever linger in our timeline—Ovi. This nameless entity, condemned by the people of Yeropa in their mythology, is filled with hatred. Only its right eye is functional while the other is scarred, hence the aforementioned name. I do wonder why we are here if we likely know what is to come, but, alas, I have been tasked by the foreign entity to help you understand what Yeropa has to offer in its parallel connection to your world.

Be wary, however, that because you are being guided, dear reader, do not fret over my commentary and humor, for I am not merely a tour guide—thank you for thinking so—and I can be rather foul at times.

…Though, frankly, wouldn’t you be curious? My, my, by all means, I would rather not play your little mind games—FREGHSTICKHT! Please, dear reader, who do you think I am? I am not a doll to poke at, nor did I think it was funny, but rather… found it entertaining, to say the least, now that I think of it.

N-No matter, let’s get back to where we left off.

Chapter 1

A Heroine’s Journey

As I said long ago to you, dear reader, you may or may not have tested my patience from the start—pity on you. I am, after all, here to guide you on what is about to happen.

Now let’s see here…Yeropa, yeropa, yeropa. Ah yes, there is our heroine! From the far eastern side of Yeropa, there lies Transcarpathia Mountain, a land that was vaguely developed in 1404, a time when leaders sent out expeditions in search of wealth and territorial expansion. Unlike in Europe, to my understanding, Yeropa was rather largely developed in the far northern side. Some places are heavily dense, but not spread across. This, however, will soon change as we venture forward. In other words, the northerners wish to contest those of the south, something that has a counterviewpoint to their ideologies.

Nevertheless, the mountain stands within the pillars of faith—a mount of faith, if you will. There is a sense of peace and harmony from nature itself. There was no need to worry about what tomorrow would bring, for even the animals and creatures live in the moment, possibly gathering food for their groups. There was no sense of “Who’s better?” or “Who’s richer?” for a lot of these folks in rural areas work in groups with no competition—a sense of synergy. Survival is a must, of course, but in the land of Transcarpathia Mountain, every step was rather a place where one could get something to eat, mainly berries, or items to be used as medicine. For a lot of the folks within, it might as well be used for agriculture! And to them, this was likely the promised land that was once suggested where the mountain of faith has nearly everything that anyone can seek and gather to one’s heart’s content.

And speaking of such, this then leads to the study of medicine and witchcraft, for nature and science seem to go rather well there. Despite no centralized townships or order in the mountain, there is rather a collection of people that come to acquaint themselves with one another. The issue? The place is somewhat considered a clash between folks of the north, and those of the south. Though most of the natives come from the south, the era of expeditions led to more Northerners migrating. In the matter of our heroine’s situation, this was no stranger than handling situations that creatures may cause some minor commotions for others. In fact, there is a sense of maintaining the harmony of faith from within one’s heart.

In that, we then come to where people come to fulfill their hope—the Shrine of Faith, the place that’s allegedly the most holy of all else. Hey, don’t take my word for it, that’s what these folks keep telling themselves! Anyways, more or less it serves as an institution for those who seek written knowledge, to write scholarly works of magic of their trade, or to simply collaborate. It is within there that a young girl is to be the one to set out on a journey. She is in her twenties, has rather long black hair that goes below the pelvis line, with a long bang that lines down near to the nose bridge, a sort of ruby-like iris that shines under the moonlight. Her name is Anastasia Velir, a rather shy though humble person that excels in faith, but is socially awkward. She can understand and sympathize with others, though speaks briefly and concisely, almost as if she’s reserved despite her interests in talking. Those that worked with her from the shrine admire her diligence and emotional intelligence whilst being curious about their faith-based science, but never really seem to truly grasp it fully and, instead, she is noted to be always working alone. This itself allows her to concentrate, but at the price of her social skills. Guess you can say that she is socially distant.

Yes, if more than anything, it does sound a lot like me from the old time, something that I once resided back in our institution, not that it really matters for my background in helping to guide you through Yeropa.

Except, where were we? Ah yes, Anastasia. Again, excelling in technical-err…emotional skills—forgive me—but poor in communication. Quite a sad life if you ask me. Nevertheless, the days within the shrine are rather dull. Instead of having the luxury of modern technologies that you lads have, they rather have themselves be talking. Come on, it’s more interesting to be talking in-person, not whatever you lads stare at. But even with this phenomenon—Wild, isn’t it?—this does not interest Anastasia. For her, seeking knowledge on the shrine’s witchcraft was more fascinating than everyday chit-chat. If anything, that’s just what today is, how yesterday went, the day before last, and so do the coming days—Like a routine, with the annoyance of interruptions at times.

Now I think of it, this only goes on for a while. Steady before, yes, but not today. You see, tasks and problems end up recurring in different commotions and emotions, so it doesn’t make our lives any more interesting. In the case of Anastasia, it doesn’t make for a more compelling tour guide. So, let’s see what would be a compelling way to go.

Aha! How about this—A wise sage from the north comes to speak of great and terrible exploits that have plagued Yeropa for a while, only to get even worse. I mean, it has been speculated that this is the work of high-end creatures striking from the night. We wouldn’t know their motivation, but soon terror may erupt! Yes, YES! Blackening the hearts of the people isn’t that more terrifying than that, or so what the sage preaches to the shrine, per se. And so, with the consultation from the shrine’s chief, many have been asked to address this impending doom, once and for all. Except, none of the wisest dared to challenge this supposed force. This was rather a matter of courage that one needed, something Anastasia would accept! I just know it in my heart, without a doubt.

“Hmm? You must be mistaken,” said Anastasia—wait WHAT?!—with a rather dumbfounded look, “I’m merely an ordinary shrine maiden..”

“Yes,” said the sage, “but no one else is willing to help. Not only that, you have an enormous power that shines within your heart, no?”

“Even if so, I have no interest in helping. I’m pretty useless, honestly, and I only do whatever my family tells me to do. I act in their interests, and it is as such that I am obligated to fulfill.”

“I see, that’s unfortunate. There is not a single bit of you that’d be interested in knowing what the world truly has to offer?”

This itself tingled her neurons. She was curious, yes, so what if the outside world has something that here doesn’t. But she was too afraid, “No, it’s too worrisome for me to go somewhere I am unfamiliar with. What if I were to get kidnapped? Or worse, what if something gets done to me, whichever it may be?”

“This is a matter of courage that one must partake.”

“Then it is something I would rather not care for. I know everything here, so I’d rather stay and live the rest of my life to whatever is deemed necessary!”

And there you go, that wraps up the tour. Absolutely abysmal, she contradicted my words! How little does she know that she will be going out no matter what! One way or another, she is going to have to accept it. Two can play at that game.

Now, let’s say she comes home one day to a surprise! Yes, she finds out that her mother has fallen suddenly ill! But wait, isn’t that too dark? Maybe I myself am that dark, but still, we got to present some serious points later on. Hmm, according to my notes…

“Improvise at will.”

FREGHSTICKHT!

In that case, Anastasia sees a rather calming tea party between her family and the sage. The house was wooden, cozy as always, and has a sense of liveliness in it. Her two cousins, small, came to greet her as usual. But to her, while she acknowledges their existence, she couldn’t help but be fixated on why in the world the sage happened to know where she lived, considering how she doesn’t recall ever inviting her colleagues to her place, let alone disclosing her residence to the sage.

“But of course, the wise sage knows many tricks up its sleeve. After all, it’s what a jester kept mocking us by calling us magicians,” said the sage before her father and the others chuckled.

“So, is that what kind of nuisance the northerners kept worrying among themselves? Quite a pity over nit-picking other people’s lives,” said her father, stroking his beard in amusement.

“Yes, that’s rather nothing out of the ordinary when you start living in a cramped space where the air’s gray—Our humors would be dry like the mechanical work we lads depend on these days.”

“Mechanics? Have the ways of witchcraft ended?”

“I’m afraid that politics would consider that blasphemy—Anyone that opposes a single ruling entity is an enemy of their ideology. I beg to differ, but that meant risking my life. Yet, here I am.”

“And you say that if our daughter could help you, then maybe our people of the Transcarpathia mountain can be saved?”

“Only time will tell, unfortunately. Right now, their numbers are far too many. For even with the deadliest magic of this fine world, they would keep sending in more men of the north. Granted that the southerners unite, if really at all, maybe this can be remedied. But, I heard that their culture would rather opt for all-out warfare. The time of peace is over, they’re coming, I’m telling you. Not only that, but this so-called group that works in the shadows may then be used as a catalyst to justify war. Lord have mercy on you if you ever get captured by them, for they know many ways of torturing people more than understanding how to handle their own people.”

If anything, the atmosphere became grim—There is a sense of tension and urgency that is being felt upon. There doesn’t seem to be any way to resolve this conflict peacefully, at least, according to the sage’s words. But, of course, everyone would have loved that there was zero effort with maximum reward—this one being peace—but that’s not how the world operates. And, as such, her father takes a moment to think. Yes, this means possibly seeing his daughter ever again, but also, the world is at stake—To save her, or save the world?

“Can you serve your family one last time, Ana?” Spoken with an ill of regret.

“Father, this is ridiculous! I’m not good enough, nor am I brave, or strong. Have pity on me, for I’m just a poor girl nobody likes,” she yells in tears, “Surely there’s got to be someone more capable than—“

“Then you are no longer welcome here anymore.”

That right there was his necessary evil—Save the world by banishing his daughter for the supposed greater good. He wished he didn’t have to do it, but, at the same time, he felt like he needed to be more direct. This sparked outrage from her family. Some sided with her outcry, and vice versa. No sides were wrong, but time is of the essence.

“Please…you can’t be serious,” Anastasia says, almost as if the world had already fallen apart.

Silence arose as dust settles in. Her father couldn’t bear to see the tears she kept holding back. For the family, it was as if she just died.

“…By my obligation…I shall faithfully serve my family until…my bitter end…”

She packed her items to travel—Mostly things that can be cooked and stored for days to come. And then? Farewell.

Hugs and kisses were exchanged, as well as the many vows of “Love you,” and “We’ll miss you,” heck even, “Please return back home safely!”

Dear reader, I regret to inform you that she’ll never come back.

But yet, as someone who never experienced a proper family, I find it rather bittersweet, lovely even. Despite their limited intellect, such as not seeing the future, they still cling onto hope that things will turn out for the better. Don’t make me laugh, humans are by definition a design flaw. This is blissful ignorance; things will get worse! But that’s what’s funny—You give these mortals our power and chaos ensues. That’s why we, dear reader, are observing their fallacy.

What? You dare to call me nihilistic?! Listen, I’ve seen things and been through a lot of scenarios to conclude that I, Maria Shalashuska, don’t see much of a difference. But then again, the foreign entity can only provide me so much wisdom besides sentience. That’s why I am actually carrying a guidebook to see what is going to happen. And, wow, would you look at that—Those jerks already left us.

Thanks a lot, dear reader.

FREGHSTICKHT!

Nevertheless, about a quarter of a mile from home stood a maiden and a sage. It was rather dull and quiet as they ventured out of the mountain. Yet, the question remains briefly, “Where are we to go?” as curious as she always is.

“The land up above will be occupied by the terrors of the north, let alone the flames of war, if they had not already done so,” said the sage sternly.

“War? Already no mercy?”

“if we don’t send out someone that represents the land of both cultures—That, be it you, I hope—then south shall become north.”

“And are you for or against them?”

“The question is not whether or not I stand with sides, but rather that our words wouldn’t affect the world’s politics. Action speaks louder than words, you know? With skin and bones, we’re only mere fools. They’ve already made up their minds, I bet you, so war is inevitable. It is, after all, part of a sine wave of life where we will need to experience the lower half before better times are up ahead. Phases and effects may change, but the principle stays the same, hence the sinus repeat in waves. You may say that things are predetermined, fate if you will. After all, like the wave, there is a sense of pattern, meaning predictability. If we can predict mindfully, then we can confidently forecast. And to that I say, if war will happen anyway, then we can try to lessen it by seeing who’s behind this commotion that will darken Yeropa’s heart, prompting doubt and resilience, hatred even. In other words, despite that things may seem to be scheduled for a downfall, we should take mindful actions, even if we don’t know what lies ahead, and, as such, Malta’s the way we’re heading—The land of the cultural exchange between the two worlds.”

“So many words, such confusion; I didn’t understand anything, but very interesting nonetheless.” She gave a heart-warming smile of innocence, a sense that maybe this whole courage against all odds will work out in the end.

Hehe, mortal fools.

Both chuckled as a way to cope with the gravity of the situation, acknowledging the fact that the sage likes to overcomplicate things. So, he attempts to simplify it,

“To put it more plainly, Yeropa can’t change with only two of us, but maybe we can try to create a ripple effect that will echo from our actions.”

“So, we’re going to change the world?”

The sage chuckled once more, “Silly for you to be this optimistic, maybe that was a bit of an exaggeration, but at least we need to tell Yeropa that we can find a compromise if we stand for one another.”

“I guess so,” She pondered his words for a moment before realizing something, “What’s your name, by the way?”

“Oh dear,” he exclaims, “I’ve forgotten that we haven’t properly introduced ourselves, really goes to show how dire this situation is. I’m Wilmert Sprachkt, fellow of the high scholars in the north. Despite its name, I’m not much of a scholar that reads the theoretical points, but rather know something more practical, per se. After all, my last assignment before graduation is to provide my master’s thesis. And what do you know, our journey is exactly what will unfold in the thesis, at least I hope so. You?”

“Ah, I’m not really anything special, actually.”

“Oh, come on, shouldn’t you feel honored that a fellow country girl is being picked up by a city-boy? I had to come all the way down to see you, after all.” He smirks.

For a moment she flushes a bit, “No no, it’s fine. It’s just me fulfilling my family’s duty. I’m seriously not that special, only a bit of magic that I know here and there, is all. They’re largely used to resolve local disputes, nothing too fancy, towards people and creatures.”

More than anything, it ended up being a back-and-forth exchange in getting acquainted. Some interesting, some funny, or many times, silence. She’s not a talker, after all, just like me—Get the job done, and call it a day.

Chapter 2

The Witch Hunt

Time flew, and the distance grew. About half a week was spent traveling on foot, covering perhaps a hundred miles or less. It wasn’t a race, but more or less an effort to keep Anastasia moving, whatever I meant by that. Though, for Wilmert, traveling was second nature. He liked walking, after all; it was kind of refreshing to walk in pitch-black smoke in the north, isn’t it? Though the area through which they traveled remained the same—forest, forest, and, you guessed it, more forest! A pity, if you ask me, though I only knew about cities of modern times, so I guess I’m not suitable to judge. It doesn’t matter anyway, dear reader, since the point was that not much had happened since I last spoke…until now.

If anything, it was a cool night, almost lukewarm. Here, a campfire was built to cook something for the night, mostly ordinary rabbits that could be found anywhere. Besides, that’s really all they could find, oddly enough.

“You don’t find it strange?” said Wilmert, in the midst of his thoughts as Anastasia roasts a rabbit, “You would think that you would see many more animals than…hares.”

“They’re rabbits, not hares,” said Anastasia with slight annoyance at hearing this. Wilmert couldn’t care less, if anything.

“But one is wilder than the other, no?”

“That’s the thing,” she looks at the meat by rotating it, which still needed to be cooked, “we have animals and creatures. You probably have seen a lot of animals in the north of Yeropa because, unlike animals, creatures are people who resonate more with nature while trying to avoid anything urban.”

“Interesting.” Wilmert took a sip of tea that was also brewed by her, “do they possess human intellect? Are there more than just these rabbit creatures?” He then realized that this would have been a good moment to write his thesis.

“Mostly rabbits and crows are the ones I’ve dealt with—here you go, gotta eat—but I wouldn’t be surprised to see others, like fish people or fox people from down south. But, to answer your question, creatures are like humans with animalistic traits. Basically, they’re like us, but with additional characteristics that we don’t have—tails for example. In theory, they should be far superior to us since they’re ‘extraordinary,’ yet they aren’t. If anything, they would rather resort to their animalistic behaviors while mimicking humans. And, hey, if they’re not aggressive, then they coexist.”

“I see, it’s rather unfortunate that we get told that these creatures are out to eliminate us all.”

Anastasia almost choked on her saliva, “What?! That’s utter nonsense!”

“Well…” He took a moment to think. “Again, remember the group we are chasing after?”

“That’s just straight up lying! Of all the creatures I dealt with back in Transcarpathia, not a single one was that utterly violent! If anything, we’re more aggressive than them, you know?”

Wilmert sighed before taking another sip, “These days, no one really bothers to question anything—If it’s loud and logical, then maybe it’s true.”

Anastasia takes a moment to think. Most of it was what she reiterated before—Creatures friendly, humans unlikely. But, maybe over there is different from back home. Something doesn’t add up, it’s got to be a misunderstanding that the Northerners cooked. But then again, if anything, maybe she was living under a rock.

As the two took a moment to reflect, a loud bang could be heard from afar—about half a mile from their camp. Then a second, a third, followed by a spectacle of fireworks in various colors illuminating the crystal, sparkling night. Clearly a fight was happening, so, without a doubt, they ran to investigate. Loudness ensues, burn marks are engraved. Two or more trees were affected. Sparks and spectacles fly with full colors; one nearly shot past Anastasia before Wilmert rescued her. In other words, it’s a whole festival of lights that replaced the audience’s joyful laughter with shouts of anger and fear.

“Come out,” said a feminine voice that was oddly cheerful, “I can take a hundred more of you freaks with these shrooms, YEE-HAW!” accompanied by another blast from what seems to be her staff.

“Is she so… high?” said Wilmert as both he and Anastasia were dumbfounded, “no sane individual would just kill for fun, unless that’s twisted coping?”

“Whatever is happening,” said Anastasia, “she’s definitely out of her mind. Do something, Wilmert!”

But before Wilmert could do something, another blast ensues, this time thrusting through their hiding spot, forcing them to dodge—they’re spotted.

“So, these lads send out the experts, huh? I guess the northerners didn’t learn their lesson hard enough, alright~!” said the feminine voice, eager to continue fighting.

This does not resonate with Anastasia, “What’s with you? Killing without purpose is pure insanity. FREGHSTICKHT! You have no business being alive!”

And for the first time ever, her years of trying to understand magic were put to use. Anastasia levitated, stretching her arms as she closed her eyes. She looks up, speaks of an ancient language, akin to Latin if it were rural-centric, dear reader.

“Saliva, sein…”

As she slowly speaks them calmly, a hex diagram appears, followed by rotary blades spinning rapidly as a charge beam enlarged. Eventually, everything seemed pitch-black as she says her final word before releasing the beam.

“…Excelsior.”

And to that, the feminine voice calmly states, “Oh shoot.”

BOOM!

The beam seemed to blast out in a radius of about a mile. And just like that, only nothingness remained afterward. Dear reader, Anastasia was fully unconscious. It was a spell on a grand scale she cast that resonated more on her rage from that supposed violent mania. Except, what caused this were creatures who annoyed that young woman’s way of life, so much so that annoying them felt like the best course of action. This would have been fine…have it not be that she smoked dried mushrooms, taking her behavior to the next level. Unfortunately, this is her old habit—an addict.

Anyway, according to the next event from our guidebook, they are then taken as prisoners—Three miles away from the blast towards southwest. Hey, at least they get a short nap. Which, fun fact—Yeropa is still largely a forest place up north, except that one island mentioned earlier. Why am I mentioning this? Because the place where they were held captive was a settlement of rabbit creatures.

About two hundred occupants live here, ruled by a scholar of the north, akin to Wilmert’s study. It has a stone fortress in an otherwise wooden huts of living. This doesn’t matter to the rabbits as they only need food, of which there was plenty—grass. And since the forest is their home, they could decide to sleep anywhere if they want. As for the purpose of the huts? That’s rather the human part of them you should be asking them. Nonetheless, time passed for who knows how long before being taken to the scholar, “Who are you all?!” It was a woman, by the way, and each was given a moment to shine, with Wilmert and Anastasia calmly stating their backgrounds. And then there’s the shroom-addict girl.

“Damn it, it’s you again—Melinda Meldilson, a witch who practiced black magic from the outskirts of northern Yeropa. I heard you abandoned your duty of segregating the cultures of the two down central, so why are you here? Have you come to accompany them to destroy Yeropa?” said the scholar, acting rather annoyed by Melinda’s presence.

“To hell with you, as if you give a damn,” said Melinda while not wanting to look at her, “I told you people many times that I’m not a witch! Witches are those of malice.”

“And what differs from what you did by performing unsanitary, unholy rituals upon yourself and others?”

“My goodness! You northerners speak like damn hypocrites, thinking with superiority complexes because of this whole culture of show and tell. I’m a priestess of the central Yeropa peninsula, formally seeing the sides of both north and south till I had enough, to which y’all send a messenger to excommunicate me because me sympathizing the ways of the south is considered ‘black magic.’”

“Those who aren’t us are against us.”

“Okay, and care to explain why these rabbit folk have no free will?”

“SILENCE!” the scholar shouted, “My reasons for dealing with them are none of your concern.”

“Yet again, the double meaning behind the northerner’s ideology of purity.”

This resulted in a mild beating from some of the rabbit lads that saw the scholar as a divine entity. Shouts, tears, and anything in association. For Anastasia, this was a shock—Creatures obeying humans? Committing violence without resistance? This was never like that back home! Creatures didn’t hold moral standpoints, at least not to her understanding, so what’s happening? An evolution? Whoever this scholar is, she needs to be taught a lesson that violence is never the answer!

“Don’t sweat it, Anastasia,” Melinda still foolishly smirks menacingly after being beaten, “she’s too powerful with their ideologies to not think twice. Mothertruckers should’ve erected her a pillar for being the most egotistical so far.” Another beating ensues.

“Wait…” Anastasia wondered, “isn’t Wilmert part of them? Can’t you do something about it?”

“Well,” Wilmert replied, “let’s just say that I, too, am rather shocked by this.”

“And you don’t get a scratch for that?” says Melinda, looking disgruntled.

Soon they were placed back in their stone jail cells. Melinda relaxed, Anastasia ponders, and Wilmert? He did not fret, for he took his journal to write out. Except, some sparks seemed to hover in and out.

“Huh,” Melinda noticed, “a pseudo-telepathic writing? Who are you trying to cryptically communicate with?”

“I’m not much of a social person, Melinda, I’m simply writing my thesis.”

“Then what is it about? My worst days being fresh out of college?” They chuckled.

“Do all scholars have to do it, Melinda?” curiously asked Anastasia.

“Heard about it, but I guess I was too cool to finish it.”

“Ouch, talk about it…”

“Never mind that, I’m bored.” Melinda got up to casually blast the cell’s wall, as if it were an ordinary Tuesday, opening a way out for them, “Just because she is one of the powerful folks doesn’t mean I have to sit here and do nothing. Come on, we gotta blast off!”

Both Wilmert and Anastasia were dumbfounded, for a second time. Nevertheless, she quickly shook her head, “Actually, I can help us go quicker. Let’s see…what’s the other spell I remember…Saliva, Sar… Specscicus!” Three phantom horses appeared, almost like clouds that illuminated in rim-light. Melinda seems rather unamused, “I’d rather run, but okay.”

And the race is on! Nothing was more important than getting out as it didn’t matter where, so long as they escaped the danger, and twenty miles per hour was the goal.

“That’s the thing!” Anastasia yelled, “If the wind were on our side, we’d be flying in no time! You know any open valleys nearby? We can use them before our horses slowly decay.”

“They’re not for everyday use?” Melinda yells back.

“No! I’d need to recast it on foot with my remaining energy! I have to concentrate after-“

A light arrow flew near Anastasia’s forehead; those rabbits had joined the fight.

“Rabbit archers—HOW?!” shouted Anastasia.

“Surprised, lad? More fun for me!” Melinda cast a few ballistic-like rain bullets. There was a sense of exchanged fire between the two, “I’ve got your back, lad,” Wilmert said in a rush, casting a shield to defend the three—finally—willing to take action after writing. Anastasia, however, didn’t know what to do. For that, she only knew magics of support and grace, nothing suited for offense. Melinda was the opposite. But, soon after, their horses started to decay, and the forest began to thin—an open valley for once! But far too late, unfortunately, as our heroes are surrounded in an arc-like formation, almost like a circle. As they take the high ground, all hope seems lost to them.

Horns blew minutes after, the ground shook with a hundred feet, if not thousands—The army of rabbits emerges. Well, they’re rabbits, after all, so they reproduce really quick, dear reader. Was this really the end? Everyone stood still, arrows being readied. Say the word, and so shall it be. Then the scholar emerged from the crowd, having a rather concerned look. Between her and Wilmert, there felt a connection of northerners’ pride before she vanished. It wasn’t clear what was said between them when combining the crowds’ grunt. Nevertheless, Wilmert told our heroes to ready themselves. Melinda was to take the offense, suggesting a deadlier rain of bullets. For him, he was to then serve as an energy bank for an endless supply of hell to be let loose. The air became thick with uncertainty before the battle commenced—A spectacle of fireworks that could be seen from miles on end.

Anastasia picks it up, which said ‘Of all the brightness of this world!’ it had a girl, long black hair, being cared with a supposed mother, and…scribbled off faces? They look like deliberate burn marks, but why? In fact, the author’s name is scribbled as well.

“Sis…does it look familiar?”

“What do you mean? You’ve never read a book, so stories were verbally told by our mother, you know?”

“And yet, there’s something important about this book, no?” Anya grabbed it to inspect the contents, but then forgets that she’s too stupid to read—illiterate—so she gave it back to her, “Sis, could you read it to me?”

“Oh? Well, let’s see…”

The story went as follows:

Big, golly, wonderful world! The place, of blue and place! Green and marvelous makes the folks go jolly! This was the time before the commoners, the time when talents united in harmony. There, a glamorous day had occurred—a child called…”

“That’s peculiar…” noted Anastasia, “the name too is scratched! Why are all these people scribbled off?”

“What do the next few pages say?” said Anya.

“Let’s see…” she turns a few, “celebration from folks around…some instances of the faceless girl wondering carelessly…huh? The rest are ripped away…”

“What does the last one say, then?”

After turning back, it seems like the last page is shown in pieces of burn marks. Lots of details are missing. From what can still be interpreted, there appears that girl, standing coldly in black marks, with the remaining description saying:

The day…so frightful…family was brightly…girl…went up in the sky…people…all around…taken…quiet, it was all quiet. The wise sage used its power to erase…”

Then, a tinnitus occurs to Anastasia, “This hearing…it’s all high…” Anya seems to be putting her hands around her head, signaling a headache, “What’s…with this?” Soon after, a sense of a red-like vibration came before closing her eyes. Something seems to be piercing inside her, but what? Dear reader, it is known that a nightmare is occurring when, for example, our bodies can’t reach for oxygen, signaling to wake up. Yet, she was breathing, so what is this to now be categorized? A living nightmare?

“Oh hey, look who came back.” There, three people, female of around Anastasia’s age, showed around this aisle, closer to the room’s entrance, with a delightful menacing look. Another of that group commented, “Oh? Why it is the quiet book girl in this town! You know very well that you’re too stupid to be part of the academia, so why are you sneaking once more?”

“Look…” frightened Anastasia, “I’ll just take my leave and-”

The one in the middle, presumably the leader, pulled her hair tightly, close to her roots, facing her, “Speakin’ off against us? My, my, that is very unlady of you!” From it, the leader forcefully pull her up, towards herself, and slam her head to one of the shelf’s frames, causing a brief blackout to Anastasia that lasted a minute.

Only tinnitus continues to comfort the black void.

When she woke up, the place was distorted—books scattered around, room with a thick smoke, and, but not the least, people screaming at the hallway, “To the gate, to the gate! Run to the center!” Anya is here, breathing heavily. Anastasia went to grab her as she tried to stand up, but was then smacked to the side from what appeared to be a shadowy figure. This figure mumbled and looked at Anya before grabbing. Instinctively, Anastasia’s immediate thought was to take a chipped frame, which was in sight on her left, yell, and stab to its head. The shadow, being somewhat solid, fell. Yet, it remained well, so it tried to strangle Anastasia with the strength of a wrestler.

Anya, sensing the strangle, yelled, “It’s in the heart! It’s the source that powers its darkness! Rip it off!”

When pinched to it, the shadow growled. Clearly this one was not smart—very simple minded. But its wounds were healing back, albeit slowly. Was this what she meant to rip it off? She isn’t a killer, and she wouldn’t dare to do it! Yet, she grew weak, so the world was starting to become even darker. Likewise, a hollow voice told her firmly, wherever and whomever it was, “To each its own; the merit of kindness only serves those who will exploit it someday. A trail cannot prematurely end—Finish what has started, or regret for eternity.” It was possibly how Sapphire said something similarly, “Use, or be used.” The red vibration overlays once more, now with a sense of weight. It is there where she remained silent and…I’d rather not say it, dear reader…Most I can allude is that the heart is a core, and when taken and crushed, the figure here serves no more…

“Envy was what came of you…”

Hm? I don’t recall this part, after numerous rehearsals…it’s the glasses girl that was among the three?!

“You weren’t really upset that I was there, but rather was afraid that I could’ve competed with you someday tenfold, so you resorted to ganging up amongst one of the strongest. Pity, because no malice was intended. Why do we humans have to be hasty to our decisions? Why do we desire to be better amongst others? This is what brews envy, amongst the seven that defies the human flaw.”

Then, a white flash came and went.

“Huh?” said Anastasia, which unveiled to be just a white blanket she had been punching—a nightmare indeed.

A moment of silence arose for her—What exactly just happened? First, her hometown was a white void, then colorful, then…dark? Dark in which the mountain of knowledge was turning into a mountain of dread? A downward despair, where much knowledge, much sorrow? Was it metaphorical? Literal? What was it?! Better yet…how did Anya know about ripping off hearts?

“It’s miraculous that the surgery went well.” Said in a feminine voice, who was coming in from a bright entrance.

“Huh?” Anastasia then remembered that she had a mark on her head…which the lines were still present on her left.

“I believe that you fell in the waters with a hard object on contact, let alone a stab to your gut. How are you feeling?” Said that woman, who is wearing a simplistic, yet a somewhat delicate outfit in design, where the shirt extends below the pelvis—likely oversized—with a white, soft headband that shows ovals and curves all around, likely a nurse by all means.

“I’m fine, maybe dizziness around. But, where are they?”

“Your friends?”

“N-No! My new family…”

“Your…new family”

“Bah! Told ye she would make it!” said in a familiar old, raspy voice—of course, it’s Sir Galwadin! Now, accompanied with Tavren and Sapphire.

Tavren was among the first to quickly approach to Anastasia, “Is everything okay?!”

“Y-Yeah!” surprised Anastasia, “I’m okay, I’m okay!”

Tavren was relieved to hear that, but appeared to have some sense of guilt. Speaking of appearance, Tavren has some noticeable bruises that would take days to heal. Sir Galwadin is being supported from his then disjointed left shoulder, and Sapphire? She looks fine, but maybe that’s because she always is covering her looks.

“Your face…” sadly spoken Tavren.

“My face?” she touches it to remind herself once more, which was patched-up. Then, checking her gut, that too was patched—around the liver area.

Sapphire notices the damages, and offers her absence whilst holding grudge. Clearly, she is mad, but why?

Sir Galwadin expresses his light nod, presumably wishing to give her some space.

“Wait!” said Anastasia, “Is something wrong about me?! Why are you also leaving?!”

“Part of my day is done for now. I err… need to consolidate on the repair costs. Take care…” There, he leaves quietly.

“But…it can’t be that my imperfections have led to dismissal, HEY!”

“Settle down…settle down…” said Tavren whilst ensuring that Anastasia didn’t make any abrupt moves.

“But…what did it all mean? Am I now a defect? My humanity is lost because now I’m a defect?!”

“Wouldn’t that make me a defect as well?”

“No…why would you call yourself like that?”

“Well, I’m morally bankrupt and physically weak altogether, no? I couldn’t stop that man from stabbing you, and what? The greatest accomplishment I did was at least pleasing a woman on one special night?”

Anastasia sighs, “Even if so, why should your pretty transgressions hold you back?”

Tavren thought for a moment, “Because no one wants a weak youngling like me?”

“Then that makes the two of us.”

Both chuckled. The same nurse came back to give her medicine for recovery, stating “Please do not stress your body too much. We only have a wooden casket to the left, so mister—would you be ever so kind to help her out?”

“By all means, I finally can once be useful!” Three together gave another chuckle. Anastasia tried getting up slowly, but it looks like it had been a while since she had stood, so Tavren was there to say “Easy! Easy! There’s no rush whatsoever!” Step by step, Tavren slowly but surely let her try to feel weight to work on—she nearly collapsed once! The nurse could only think about how kind the boy is as she then tries to clean up the place a bit, after the surgery. Likewise, there was no doorway, just a huge gap, so the two easily got out. When outside, the sun was already settling down towards the south, revealing below small, steep steps that they had to descend towards a small, illuminating town place.

“Well,” said Anastasia, “I suppose that we have a long way to go, huh?”

Tavren acknowledges it with a nod. Both agreed that it’ll likely be night time when they get to the town. Tavren takes the first step, then waits for her on the second. Rinse and repeat.

By then, they had reached the island before Yerusalem—Saepris, the alternate island of your real world counterpart “Cyprus,” and the opposite of Kurskolin.
